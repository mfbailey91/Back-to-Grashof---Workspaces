**Project:** Characterization of Manipulator Workspaces by Kinematic Decomposition  
**Purpose:** State the general framework beyond the shorthand phrase “Grashof criterion”

## 1. Central distinction

This project does not begin with the premise that a universal Grashof inequality should exist for every manipulator.

It begins with the fixed-position inverse-kinematic problem.

For forward kinematics

\[
g(q)=\bigl(p(q),R(q)\bigr),
\]

the fixed-position fiber at a task point \(p^*\) is

\[
\mathcal F_{p^*}
=
\{q\in Q:p(q)=p^*\}.
\]

Its full orientation image is

\[
\mathcal O(p^*)
=
\{R(q):q\in\mathcal F_{p^*}\}
\subseteq SO(3),
\]

or the corresponding declared quotient task image, such as a pointing image in \(S^2\).

Fixing position may be represented by an exact virtual closed source mechanism. The scientific problem is then to determine the global task image generated by that source mechanism.

---

## 2. Why four-bars appear

In the planar 3R reference problem, fixing the Cartesian tool point creates a one-degree-of-freedom planar four-bar. Full planar orientation coverage becomes complete rotation of a designated virtual link.

The logical direction is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{fixed-position planar 3R}
\Rightarrow
\text{virtual planar 4R}
\Rightarrow
\text{designated-link rotation coverage}
\Rightarrow
\text{Grashof-type analysis}.
}
\]

It is not:

\[
\text{Grashof works for four-bars}
\Rightarrow
\text{force every spatial manipulator into one four-bar rule}.
\]

Even in the planar case, conventional Grashof classification, inversion identity, assemblability, circuit, and designated-link rotatability are distinct pieces of information. A Grashof link set may realize a double-rocker inversion in which the task-designated link does not rotate fully.

Therefore the relevant abstraction is not “the Grashof flag.” It is a **mechanism behavior certificate**.

---

## 3. General evidence chain

```text
source open chain
  -> fixed-position problem
  -> exact virtual closure
  -> source parent/fiber and task image
  -> architecture- or task-derived child family
  -> child lift/equivalence certificate
  -> mechanism behavior certificate
  -> coverage and compatibility stitching
  -> independent source-parent task-image validation
  -> workspace classification
```

A source mechanism may also be analyzed directly, in which case the decomposition steps are omitted. What may not be omitted is the connection from the mechanism result to the source manipulator and the declared task image.

---

## 4. Mechanism behavior is broader than Grashof classification

A four-bar or lower-dimensional closed mechanism may need to report several behaviors:

- assemblability;
- mechanism family and role-aware joint sequence;
- inversion, circuit, and branch structure;
- designated input/output coordinate;
- complete rotation or bounded rocking;
- winding number or angular coverage;
- task-image curve or interval;
- change-point and singular states;
- connected-component completeness;
- joint-limit truncation when limits are part of the declared problem;
- analytical proof status;
- numerical continuation status;
- sampling resolution, confidence, and out-of-distribution status;
- exact-solver fallback result.

Classical Grashof classification is one field in this larger record for planar 4R mechanisms. A family-specific spatial Grashof-like rule may eventually supply another field. Neither term should replace the behavior actually required by the task.

## 6. Child families

Suppose a higher-dimensional fixed-position parent is parameterized or sliced by \(\lambda\in\Lambda\), producing child sets

\[
\mathcal F_{p^*,\lambda}
\subseteq
\mathcal F_{p^*}.
\]

Let the declared task map be

\[
h:\mathcal F_{p^*}\rightarrow\mathcal T,
\]

where \(\mathcal T\) may be \(SO(2)\), \(S^2\), \(SO(3)\), or another explicitly declared task space.

The child task image is

\[
\mathcal O_\lambda(p^*)
=
h\bigl(\mathcal F_{p^*,\lambda}\bigr).
\]

A useful child family must be grounded in the source problem. Examples include:

- an exact architecture-derived compound-joint reduction;
- a task-level set with a stated physical meaning;
- a symmetry quotient with a verified reconstruction map;
- another explicit slicing constraint whose lift and tangent relation to the parent are validated.

An arbitrary coordinate freeze may be a diagnostic slice, but it is not automatically a canonical mechanism decomposition.

---

## 7. Coverage stitching

The parent task image is not established merely by finding favorable behavior in selected children.

The intended reconstruction is

\[
\mathcal O(p^*)
=
\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
\mathcal O_\lambda(p^*),
\]

but this equality is a theorem obligation or a numerical validation target, not a definition that may be assumed.

A valid coverage-stitching claim requires the following gates.

### 7.1 Family coverage

The declared child family must cover the relevant parent components or state exactly which subset it covers:

\[
\mathcal F_{p^*}^{\mathrm{declared}}
=
\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
\mathcal F_{p^*,\lambda}.
\]

Missing parameter intervals, branches, singular transitions, or disconnected components must be reported.

### 7.2 Lift and equivalence

Every child mechanism must map back to the source parent through a validated lift. Depending on the claim, checks may include:

- pose equality;
- closure equality;
- tangent/null-space agreement;
- inactive-coordinate locking;
- fixed mechanism dimensions;
- branch correspondence;
- global motion equivalence;
- task-map equality.

Local tangent agreement alone does not prove global child equivalence.

### 7.3 Component preservation

The construction must preserve or explicitly map source components, child circuits, and branch transitions. Combining images from incompatible assemblies can create a false appearance of coverage.

### 7.4 Neighbor compatibility

Neighboring child images must meet or transition according to the source parent geometry. Overlap, endpoint continuation, change points, and singular transitions must be checked rather than inferred from proximity in a plot.

### 7.5 Task-map consistency

All children must use the same declared task map and frame conventions. A mixture of tool-roll coordinates, pointing directions, solver coordinates, or cyclic joint origins cannot be stitched without an explicit transformation.

### 7.6 Completeness and uncertainty propagation

The parent result inherits the weakest relevant child status. A budget-limited or unresolved family region prevents an unqualified complete-coverage claim unless another certified route covers that region.

### 7.7 Independent comparison

The reconstructed image must be compared with a decomposition-free source-parent reference. Agreement should be tested for:

- set coverage;
- component count;
- boundary location;
- multiplicity where relevant;
- singular/change-point structure;
- orientation or pointing metrics;
- refinement stability.

The decomposition is credited only after this comparison.

## 8. Dexterity and reduced tasks

For planar full orientation,

\[
\mathcal O(p^*)=SO(2)
\]

is the dexterity condition.

For spatial full orientation,

\[
\mathcal O(p^*)=SO(3)
\]

is the dexterity condition.

For a declared pointing task,

\[
\mathcal P(p^*)=S^2
\]

may define a pointing-complete workspace, but it is not full dexterity unless roll is restored through a verified symmetry and reconstruction contract.

An aligned terminal-roll factorization must establish, at minimum:

- position invariance under terminal roll;
- pointing invariance under terminal roll;
- sufficient roll range;
- quotient validity;
- reconstruction of full orientation;
- component correspondence;
- treatment of joint limits.

## 9. Analytical and numerical predicates

The framework remains mechanism-based even when a closed-form family predicate is unavailable.

Analytical structure may include:

- fixed-position fiber definition;
- virtual closure construction;
- mobility/dimension count;
- task quotient;
- child-family definition;
- lift and equivalence conditions;
- coverage-stitching criterion.

The final behavior predicate may be:

- a proven analytical inequality;
- exact numerical continuation or root solving;
- a conservative numerical atlas;
- a surrogate with confidence, out-of-distribution detection, and exact fallback.

A careful description is:

> A mechanism-based workspace criterion whose structural reduction and coverage contract are analytical, while one or more family-specific mechanism behaviors may be evaluated numerically.

`Semi-analytical` is appropriate only when the analytical and numerical portions, uncertainty, and fallback path are stated separately.

## 10. Relationship to the decomposition ladder

```text
L3 planar 3R
  fixed-position mobility 1
  target SO(2)
  direct planar four-bar behavior

L4 spatial 4R
  fixed-position mobility 1
  target orientation curve in SO(3)
  direct one-DOF source behavior

L5 spatial 5R
  fixed-position mobility 2
  target pointing image in S^2
  parameterized one-DOF child families plus stitching

L6 spatial 6R
  fixed-position mobility 3
  target SO(3)
  nested or otherwise structured child families plus full reconstruction

L7 spatial 7R
  fixed-position mobility 4
  target SO(3) plus redundancy/gauge
  orientation reconstruction separated from self-motion structure
```

Dimensional possibility does not guarantee coverage. The ladder identifies the source object and the reconstruction problem at each rung.

---

## 11. Current scientific interpretation

The trusted planar result demonstrates that a mechanism behavior can exactly characterize dexterity when the source reduction and task correspondence are exact.

The current spatial results demonstrate that:

- fixed-position source mechanisms and task-derived local fibers can be constructed;
- a valid local fiber is not automatically a spatial four-bar;
- topology-derived or fixed-axis candidate reductions may fail while the source-closure framework remains valid;
- failure of a child hypothesis is evidence about the decomposition, not a refutation of fixed-position analysis;
- the missing central operation is not simply “find the spatial Grashof rule,” but **construct legitimate child families and prove their coverage stitching**.
